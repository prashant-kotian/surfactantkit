The SMILES contains a singly charged sulfate headgroup, [OS(=O)(=O)[O-]], with one sodium counterion. The surfactant is therefore a 1:1 anionic surfactant.

CMC from the observed break
---------------------------

Because concentrations double at every step, define

    x = log2(c / 0.40577109375 mM),

so the eight measurements have x = 0,...,7.

The local pre-break segment immediately below the transition, using 3.24617 and 6.49234 mM, is

    gamma = 89.6634 - 11.6968 x.

A least-squares fit to the three post-break points, 12.9847-51.9387 mM, gives

    gamma = 40.75167 - 0.56150 x.

Their intersection is

    x_CMC = 4.39249,
    c_CMC = 0.40577109375(2^4.39249) = 8.52 mM.

This is an operational tensiometric CMC, not an exact thermodynamic singularity. The raw observations only bracket the transition between 6.492 and 12.985 mM. Including the preceding point in the pre-CMC regression moves the intersection to approximately 9.32 mM, so a reasonable method-sensitivity range is about 8.5-9.3 mM. No statistical confidence interval can be obtained without replicate measurements or measurement uncertainties.

The fitted intersection surface tension is approximately 38.29 mN/m. The final three measurements average 37.383 mN/m, although their small residual downward slope shows that the measured post-CMC region is not perfectly flat.

Apparent maximum surface excess
-------------------------------

The steepest measured interval wholly below the estimated CMC is 3.24617-6.49234 mM:

    Delta gamma / Delta ln(c)
      = (42.8762 - 54.5730) / ln(2)
      = -16.8749 mN/m.

For a fully dissociated 1:1 ionic surfactant without added supporting electrolyte, the ideal-dilute Gibbs equation has the factor 2:

    Gamma = -(1 / 2RT)(d gamma / d ln(c)).

Therefore,

    Gamma_max,app
      = 0.0168749 / [2(8.3144626)(296.15)]
      = 3.43e-6 mol/m2.

The corresponding apparent molecular area is

    A_min,app = [1 / (N_A Gamma_max)](1e18) = 0.485 nm2.

These are finite-difference, concentration-based estimates. A strictly thermodynamic, activity-corrected Gamma_max would require ionic activity coefficients and a sufficiently dense curve to evaluate the derivative near saturation.

What cannot be identified
-------------------------

A unique adsorption isotherm cannot be assigned. The increasingly steep sub-CMC curve is inconsistent with a simple ideal Langmuir/Szyszkowski description over the entire measured range—for example, the first two surface-tension drops on successive concentration doublings are 1.9223 and 5.0444 mN/m, a ratio of 2.62, whereas an ideal Langmuir curve cannot accelerate by more than a factor of two under a doubling. This suggests cooperative or other nonideal adsorption, but it does not specifically prove a Frumkin isotherm. Other equations of state, ionic nonideality, impurities, or experimental effects can produce similar curvature. Consequently, a Frumkin interaction parameter is not uniquely recoverable.

The standard free energy of micellization is also not determined by CMC alone for an ionic surfactant. Its calculation requires a defined standard state, activity coefficients, and especially the micellar counterion-binding or degree-of-ionization parameter, none of which are supplied by the SMILES or tensiometry data.

Aggregation number, micelle size and shape, counterion binding, Krafft temperature, micellization enthalpy, and micellization entropy likewise require additional measurements.
